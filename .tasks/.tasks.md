- [DONE: 2026-08-30] [est: 26000] **A write that the store rejects now says so, through the notice a refused hydrate already used** — the gate covers a session that starts broken; a device that fills up WHILE THE APP IS OPEN closes no gate at all, so `usePersistedBlob` and the nine inline `setItem` catches reported to Sentry and left the screen unchanged while the user's edits stopped landing. Twelve call sites, and nine of them are in modules with no React in them (`lib/tombstones.ts`, `lib/sync-cursors.ts`, `lib/currency-rates.ts`), so "have the caller raise a toast" was available at three of them.

  **`observeStorageFailures(observer)` is the seam, and it fires BEFORE the budget check.** The once-per-site/keyspace budget exists to keep a full disk from becoming a stream of Sentry events; an observer has its own idea of repetition, and gating it behind the same check would let a READ that spent a pair's entry hide the first WRITE from the user entirely. A case pins that ordering, and moving the call after the check turns exactly that case red.

  **The observer filters to `.setItem` and the filter is a decision, not a detail.** A currency preference or a language that could not be read costs the user a default, not their data — and the five reads that DO cost data are already covered by the gate half. Dropping the filter makes "changes aren't being saved" fire because `locale-helpers.getItem` threw, which is false; a case says so.

  **Renamed `hydration-gate-notice` → `storage-notice`, because the module now answers a bigger question.** `useStorageNotice(gateRefusing)` keeps the per-provider predicate (the write half needs no argument — it arrives through the registry) and the two i18n strings were rewritten in all six locales from "could not be read" to "is not available", which is true of both causes. One latch, so a device that fails both ways says it once.

  **Six new cases on the hook and two end-to-end.** A rejected write raises it; a rejected read does not; a spent budget does not suppress it; a throwing observer does not take a storage `.catch()` down with it. `PremiumProvider` with a failing `setItem` raises exactly this toast after an activation, and the same provider with a working one raises nothing — the case that keeps the other from being vacuous.

  **The unsubscribe is asserted on the registry rather than through a React unmount**, and the comment says why: the render harness has no unmount phase, so a case that dropped the probe from the tree would pass whether or not the effect returned its cleanup at all.
